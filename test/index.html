On Reading Slowly

When reading a physical book, the subtle action of placing a finger beneath a line of text serves two functions: it keeps your focus and it sets your pace. This act is often subconscious, yet it ties you to the flow of the story.

Digital text tends to be read in bursts. The eye jumps ahead, skims a linked phrase, and lands somewhere further down the page with only a vague sense of what came before. A moving cursor can bring back some of that tactile rhythm.

  • Letters just behind the cursor rise a little, then settle back.
  • Only the paragraph under the cursor is changed, so long pages stay fast.
Reading is to the mind what exercise is to the body — and pacing matters for both.
